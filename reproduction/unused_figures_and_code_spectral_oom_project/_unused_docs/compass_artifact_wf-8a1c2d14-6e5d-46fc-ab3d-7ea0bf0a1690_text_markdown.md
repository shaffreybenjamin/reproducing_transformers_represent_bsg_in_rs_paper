# Technical Assessment: Three Proposed Improvements to the Spectral-OOM Belief-Geometry Estimator

## TL;DR
- **Suggestion 1 (direct multi-step readouts) is the strongest and should be implemented; Suggestion 2 (whitening) is right in spirit but misstated, and only becomes the correct Hsu–Kakade–Zhang estimator when fused with Suggestion 1 — together they reconstruct a reduced-rank-regression / CCA estimator, which is the principled fix.** Suggestion 3a (activation clustering) is the only proposal that truly escapes output-anchoring and is worth building as an independent cross-check; Suggestion 3b (N4SID/MOESP) is either subsumed by 1+2 (its CVA variant) or, as literally stated, a category error.
- **The RRXOR gap is estimator-limited, not intrinsic.** The belief distinctions are present in the output distribution — just not at horizon 1; the next-token R²=0.31 is a length-1 statement, and RRXOR's degenerate beliefs separate in their conditional future distributions at horizon ≈2–3. The information is also demonstrably present in the activations (recoverable from the concatenation of layers). So an output-anchored method *can* recover the geometry if it reads futures directly at sufficient horizon and ranks directions by predictive rather than absolute variance.
- **Both critic caveats are correct.** Rollout collapse is a faithful property of the true (nilpotent, transient-state) mixed-state dynamics, not a bug; forcing apex regeneration would be overfitting. Label-free order selection via bootstrap stability / singular-value SNR / operator-consistency is standard and sound — with the precision that the target dimension is ~5 (the linear belief dimension), not 36 (the count of distinct belief points).

## Key Findings

**1. What Hsu–Kakade–Zhang actually do — and why it matters for Suggestions 1 and 2.** The HKZ algorithm is *not* a plain SVD of a composed observability matrix, and it does *not* whiten against raw observation variance. In their own words (arXiv:0811.4413, JCSS 2012), it "at its core is a singular value decomposition (SVD) of a correlation matrix between past and future observations. This SVD can be viewed as a Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) (Hotelling, 1935) between past and future observations." Their observable operators are formed *directly from empirical joint-probability tables* — pairs and triples of observations — not by composing estimated single-step operators: the operator is B(η) = (UᵀTriples(η)V)(UᵀPairs V)⁻¹, where U,V are the top singular vectors of the past–future correlation. The U matrix (top left singular vectors of the past–future correlation/Hankel matrix) supplies a low-dimensional coordinate system in which the operators are well-conditioned; it is obtained from *correlation between past and future*, i.e., it scores directions by shared past–future (predictive) variance, not by absolute variance. HKZ explicitly note this construction *avoids* Jaeger's "characteristic events" assumption — the whitening/SVD step replaces it.

**2. CCA = reduced-rank regression between whitened variables; subspace-ID's CVA variant is the same object.** It is a classical result (Izenman 1975; De la Torre 2012) — recently re-formulated for the high-dimensional regime by Donnat & Tuzhilina, "Canonical Correlation Analysis as Reduced Rank Regression in High Dimensions" (arXiv:2405.19539, 2024: "we begin by expliciting a link between reduced rank regression (RRR) and CCA … this connection has already been recognized in the existing literature [Izenman, 1975, De la Torre, 2012]") — that CCA is exactly reduced-rank regression between whitened variables, with canonical correlations playing the role of regression coefficients. In subspace identification, the canonical-variate-analysis (CVA / Larimore) algorithm chooses the state by CCA of past and future, solved by a generalized SVD weighted by the past and future covariances — identical in form to HKZ. N4SID/MOESP build a block-Hankel embedding and take a truncated SVD of the projection of futures onto pasts.

**3. Classical N4SID/MOESP assume state-independent additive noise — which disqualifies the textbook versions for this problem.** HKZ are explicit (arXiv:0811.4413): subspace-ID "algorithms presented in the literature cannot be directly used to learn HMMs because they assume additive noise models with noise distributions independent of the underlying states, and such models are not suitable for HMMs (an exception is (Andersson et al., 2003))." This is the precise sense in which a naïve N4SID/MOESP application is a category error here.

**4. RRXOR structure (primary sources).** Per Shai, Marzen, Teixeira, Gietelink Oldenziel & Riechers, "Transformers Represent Belief State Geometry in their Residual Stream" (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.15943), and the computational-mechanics literature it cites: the data-generating ε-machine has 5 recurrent causal states; the mixed-state presentation has 36 belief states (5 recurrent + 31 transient), living in a 4-simplex (belief vectors are 5-dimensional, affine dimension 4). The next-token degeneracy statistic R²=0.31 is a *length-1* (next-token) quantity — the paper's Fig. 7 reports "low correlation for RRXOR (R²=0.31) … indicating that RRXOR belief state geometry is not captured by next-token predictions alone." In computational mechanics, distinct causal/belief states are by definition distinguished by P(future word | state) at some finite horizon, and RRXOR (with its deterministic every-third-token XOR) resolves at horizon ≈2–3. The MSP transition operators are nondiagonalizable/defective with a nilpotent transient block; the transient belief states are synchronizing points with no attractor of their own (the only attractor is the recurrent 5-state ε-machine).

## Details

### Suggestion 1 — Direct multi-step readouts: SOUND, IMPLEMENT.
This is correct and is genuinely closer to the HKZ/Hankel/PSR construction than composing operators. The spectral-learning Hankel matrix is built from *empirical joint statistics of pasts and futures*; the PSR literature calls multi-step future-token distributions "features of the future" and fits the state by linear regression onto them directly (Boots–Gordon PSTD; Hefny et al.). Stacking [C₁ | C₂ | … | C_L], where C_k is the ridge regression of activations onto the empirically measured length-k future-token distribution P(x_{t+1:t+k}|w), is exactly the data-driven observability/Hankel block — and each C_k remains linear in belief (it is b(w)T_{x1}…T_{xk}·1 read out of activations) and label-free.

Two structural pathologies of the composed-operator construction are removed: (i) multiplicative error compounding through the estimated {A_x}, and (ii) the funneling of every observable through the single readout C, which biases the recovered subspace toward the next-token direction. The writeup's finding that longer horizons via *composed* operators do not help (best at L=3) does *not* generalize to direct readouts, and the two should be expected to differ sharply: extending horizon by composition adds noise-amplified copies of the same C-rooted, next-token-biased direction, whereas a direct length-k readout injects genuinely new empirical information P(x_{t+1:t+k}) that is not a function of C. Because RRXOR's degenerate beliefs separate at future horizon ≈2–3, direct length-2 and length-3 readouts should supply precisely the discriminating signal the composed tower failed to carry. This is the canonical causal-state construction (states distinguished by their future morphs) transposed into activation coordinates; it is the single most principled change.

The one honest qualification: this does not "escape" output-anchoring in the literal sense — it is still built from the token/output distribution. But that is the *right* anchor, because the belief distinctions genuinely live in the future-token distribution at horizon ≥2. The degeneracy is only in the horizon-1 marginal. Anchoring to sufficiently long futures is not a workaround; it is the definition of a causal state.

### Suggestion 2 — Whitening: PARTLY MISSTATED; correct only when fused with Suggestion 1.
The critic's *intent* — rank directions by belief-relevant variance rather than absolute variance, so faint belief-only directions are not crowded out by loud next-token directions — is exactly the spirit of HKZ. But the specific prescription "generalized/whitened SVD against the activation covariance" is an imprecise description of what HKZ whiten. HKZ whiten the *past–future cross-correlation* by *both* the past covariance and the future covariance; the belief-relevant scoring comes from correlation with the future, not from equalizing activation (past) variance. Whitening against the activation covariance *alone* is just PCA-whitening of the inputs: it equalizes activation directions but, applied to the current composed-operator O, can actively *hurt* by amplifying low-variance noise directions in activation space, since nothing ties those directions to belief relevance. The belief-relevant rebalancing only materializes when there is an explicit *future* block to whiten against — which requires Suggestion 1. In short: Suggestion 2 is the correct idea attached to the wrong covariance; it is not independently implementable as "whiten O against Σ_activation."

### Suggestions 1 + 2 together = CCA / reduced-rank regression: IMPLEMENT AS A UNIT.
Combining direct future readouts (Suggestion 1) with whitening of the past–future cross-covariance (Suggestion 2 done correctly) is exactly reduced-rank regression / CCA between activation-space pasts and empirical future-token statistics. This is a principled, classical, statistically consistent estimator — the HKZ / Boots–Gordon spectral-learning construction expressed in activation coordinates, and identical in form to the CVA subspace-ID estimator. This fusion, not either suggestion alone, is the correct version of the method, and is the primary recommendation.

### Suggestion 3a — Activation cluster separation: USEFUL as an independent compass; build it as a cross-check.
This is the only proposal that truly escapes output-anchoring, because it uses activation geometry alone. With 36 discrete belief states, activations should cluster, and the linear subspace that best separates the clusters recovers belief geometry with no reference to the softmax. Its value is precisely that it is an *orthogonal* source of evidence to the output-anchored estimator. Failure modes are real: (i) unsupervised clustering in high-dimensional activation space is fragile, and the 31 transient states are close together and may not separate cleanly; (ii) the number of clusters must be chosen — the same order-selection problem, now circular with the cluster count; (iii) "separable" is not identical to "belief-relevant" — clusters may reflect position or other features; (iv) it does not generalize to continuous processes such as Mess3 (the critic concedes this). Verdict: worth implementing as a diagnostic and consistency check against the 1+2 estimator, not as the primary recovery method.

### Suggestion 3b — N4SID/MOESP subspace ID: DO NOT implement as a separate method.
As literally stated, applying textbook N4SID/MOESP with activation-as-state and token-as-output is unsound: those algorithms assume a linear-Gaussian state-space model with additive noise independent of the state, which HKZ explicitly flag as inapplicable to HMMs; the token emission is categorical and the belief update is the nonlinear projective Bayesian map, not additive Gaussian noise. The *sound* core of 3b is the CVA variant — but CVA is CCA of past and future, i.e., it collapses exactly into Suggestions 1+2 and offers nothing additional. Moreover, the framing "identify the state from the transition structure of activations rather than outputs alone" is partly illusory: the transformer is deterministic given its input, so activation dynamics are a readout of the token history, not independent information; and N4SID's notion of "state" is itself *defined* by predicting future outputs, so a faithful subspace-ID still routes through outputs and recovers only output-relevant structure. The genuinely non-output-routed idea is 3a, not 3b.

### Question 6 — Intrinsic vs. estimator-limited: ESTIMATOR-LIMITED.
Three facts settle this. (i) The information is in the activations: per Shai et al. (arXiv:2405.15943), for RRXOR the geometry "is not represented in any of the individual layers of the transformer, but is represented in the concatenation of the layers" — "individual layers did not fully represent the geometry, but concatenating activations across all layers yielded a veridical representation" (consistent with the writeup's supervised R²=1.00 on concatenated activations). (ii) The information is in the outputs too — just not at horizon 1; causal states are distinguished by their future distributions, and RRXOR's degeneracy is confined to the length-1 marginal, with separation appearing at horizon ≈2–3. (iii) The current method fails for two diagnosable reasons — operator composition (error compounding + C-funneling) and plain-SVD ranking by absolute variance — both of which Suggestions 1 and 2 address. The critic's premise behind Suggestion 3 ("if belief distinctions are never well-expressed in the output distribution at any horizon") is therefore false for RRXOR; the distinctions *are* expressed at horizon ≥2. This weakens the motivation for a non-output signal (3b) and strengthens the case that the clean fix is the CCA/RRR estimator reading futures at the synchronization horizon. (A subtle residual error source: the method uses the network's own softmax as the normalizer, which is the network's belief, not the true process belief; for direct multi-step readouts one would use the enumerated true future statistics for toy processes, removing this mismatch.)

### Caveat A — Rollout collapse is not a bug: CORRECT.
The MSP transient operators are nilpotent/defective and the 31 transient belief states are synchronizing points with no attractor; the only attracting set is the recurrent 5-state ε-machine. Free rollout from the root therefore *should* collapse onto the recurrent set — that is the true dynamics. Forcing free rollout to regenerate the transient apexes would fit a dynamics that does not match the process (transients are visited only during synchronization from the start distribution, never regenerated in steady state), i.e., overfitting. The proposed better tests — anchoring at multiple seed points and scoring one-step consistency (η → ηTˣ/normalizer) — test the operator locally without demanding a spurious attractor, and are the right diagnostics.

### Caveat B — Order selection via bootstrap stability: CORRECT and standard.
Reading d=5 off the known state count is indeed an honesty problem rather than an accuracy problem, and it is separable from accuracy. The proposed label-free criteria are established practice: in spectral/OOM and Markov-state-model learning, one discards singular values whose bootstrap signal-to-noise ratio falls below a threshold; subspace-ID uses information-criterion-type tests on Hankel singular values; and operator-consistency (does the recovered d-dimensional model reproduce held-out joint statistics) is a standard validation. One precision point the writeup should absorb: the target dimension is the *linear belief dimension* (~5, or affine 4), not the count of 36 distinct belief points. The absence of a clean elbow at 36 is expected — 36 is the number of points, while the SVD rank reflects the 5-dimensional subspace they occupy. Order selection should be calibrated to find ~5, and bootstrap stability should reveal a stable 5-dimensional subspace.

## Recommendations
1. **Implement Suggestions 1 and 2 jointly as a single CCA / reduced-rank-regression estimator** between activations (past side) and stacked empirical future-token distributions [C₁|…|C_L] (future side), whitening the past–future cross-covariance by both covariances. This is the HKZ/CVA construction in activation coordinates and is the principled fix. **Benchmark:** RRXOR unsupervised state-level R² should rise materially above the current 0.82, and the belief-vs-next-token distance preservation should flip back toward the supervised ordering (belief distances better preserved than next-token).
2. **Set the future horizon to the synchronization depth (≈3 for RRXOR), not longer.** Test L=2,3,4 with direct readouts; expect the gain to arrive by L≈3 and then plateau — distinct from the composed-operator result where horizon never helped. **Threshold to change course:** if direct readouts at L=3 do *not* improve over composed operators, the bottleneck is estimation noise in C_k (sample size / enumeration fidelity), not structure — revisit the regression targets before abandoning the approach.
3. **Build Suggestion 3a as an independent diagnostic**, not the primary method: cluster activations, take the subspace spanning cluster structure, and compare its recovered geometry to the 1+2 estimator. Agreement between an output-anchored estimator and an activation-only estimator is strong evidence of veridical recovery. **Do not** rely on it for Mess3 or other continuous processes.
4. **Do not implement Suggestion 3b** as a separate track. If subspace-ID is desired, use only its CVA form — which is already Recommendation 1.
5. **Adopt bootstrap-stability + operator-consistency order selection**, calibrated to the linear belief dimension (~5), and report it instead of d set by hand. Keep the rollout test as multi-seed anchoring + one-step consistency; do not "fix" apex regeneration.

## Caveats
- The horizon-≈3 disambiguation figure for RRXOR is derived from the computational-mechanics literature (history-K=3 / future-L=2–3 analyses and finite Markov order), not stated verbatim in the belief-geometry paper; treat it as a well-supported estimate, not a quoted constant.
- The claim that the RRXOR MSP operators satisfy a specific nilpotency (e.g., T₁⁵=0) is strongly implied by the nondiagonalizable-metadynamics theory (transient block nilpotent, finite synchronization) but I did not find it stated as a verbatim RRXOR-specific equation in the cited papers; the qualitative claim (nilpotent/defective, transient, no attractor) is firmly established.
- "Belief-relevant variance" recovery via CCA inherits CCA's known fragility in high dimensions: with d_model and stacked future blocks both large relative to sample size, the canonical directions can be inconsistent without regularization — ridge/regularized CCA is advisable, and the existing ridge regressions already point that way.
- All quantitative recovery predictions assume the toy-process futures are enumerated exactly; with sampled futures, finite-sample noise in the higher C_k blocks is the dominant risk and should be monitored via the bootstrap SNR of the corresponding singular directions.
